Swipey · UX audit · 16 Aug 2026

The foundations are right. What's missing is confirmation.

Six independent audits swept the app — the deck loop, home and navigation, the commit flow, the bin, first-run and error states, accessibility and craft. Almost every high-value finding turned out to be the same shape: the user does something, and Swipey doesn't tell them it landed. A flick that doesn't commit. A swipe that reveals bare canvas. An Undo that buzzes and does nothing. A system dialog nobody warned them about. Twenty minutes of marks that a phone call erases.

Fix confirmation and the app stops feeling like a prototype — without changing a single screen's structure.

The one to do first

The swipe threshold is calibrated for a 60 Hz phone. Yours is 120.

SwipeCard.kt:256 estimates fling velocity as dragAmount * 60f. On a 120 Hz display each frame's delta is half as large, so the FlingVelocity = 1500f gate is effectively doubled and roughly half your quick flicks snap back instead of committing. It also samples only the final frame, so a flick that eases off reads as near-zero.

You experience this as the app's only gesture inexplicably refusing.

It beats the runners-up on every axis: about an hour of work, a one-for-one swap with no design decision attached, it affects every interaction in the app rather than one screen, and it fixes something that currently reads as “this app is broken” rather than “this app is missing a feature.” Prefetching is felt more often but costs a day; the permission copy saves more installs but only for people who tap the wrong option.

05

Don't lose the session to a phone call

Process death during a call returns you to a deck whose counter has reset from 300/540 to 1/380 and whose marked chip is simply gone. Sixty decisions, unannounced.

Only KEEP is persisted (DeckViewModel.kt:121); every mark lives in an in-memory map. Persist MARK the same way: drop the keep && guard, add a decision = 'MARK' query to ReviewedMediaDao, re-hydrate SwipeSession.marked in load().

Critical: the Discard path at DeckScreen.kt:117 must delete that session's MARK rows, or discarded marks silently re-appear on the next load. The narrower version — createSavedStateHandle() holding marked ids and position — buys most of this for a fraction of the work and is the sane first cut.

Ship-together packages

Four groups where the pieces are worth more together than apart.

Deck feel — do this first

Items 1, 2, 8, plus the control-strobe fix. Individually they read as small; together they turn the deck from twitchy to solid.

Honest disclosure, better timed

Item 3, plus item 12's rewording, item 16's Bin subtitle, and the disclosure labels. Every one makes a required claim more visible; none softens anything. One pass through Copy.kt and DisclosureSheet.kt.

Recoverability at scale

Items 11, 12, 13, plus the Bin header and empty state. Select-all is what makes the multi-confirmation warning necessary; the restore-outcome split is what makes a failed batch actionable.

Kept items are visible again

Items 9 and 14. Fourteen supplies the remainingCount that 9's Home-side story depends on, and they share the albumDone string.

Already solid — leave alone

The custom design system holds. Zero material3 imports in ui/, and every proposal above builds out of SwipeyButton, SwipeyChip, SwipeyRow, SwipeyText, SwipeyProgressBar and the existing tokens.

The disclosure corpus is correct and verbatim. The problem is timing and hierarchy, never content — nothing in this list shortens or hides a required sentence.

The safety architecture is genuinely good: the verifyAndResolve() reconcile pass, the per-item IS_TRASHED re-check behind RecoveryReport, the 500-URI chunking, the DeleteRecordAndReview resolution that correctly re-enables a restored item for swiping, and the total absence of any permanent-delete path.

DeckScreen's Back-with-marks discard dialog is the right pattern — Review just needs to copy it. Home's hero is working; don't stack new rows above it. The deck's decision to wrap its own chrome in SwipeyDarkColors is right; only the system's half of that surface was missed. And DeckMessage's deliberate refusal to auto-navigate on the nothing-marked branch is the correct instinct — item 6 just extends it to the case that matters more.

Six parallel audits, each followed by an adversarial verifier whose job was to kill findings that were already implemented, wrongly cited, or that would have broken the app's honest-disclosure rules. 51 findings survived; 6 were killed.
